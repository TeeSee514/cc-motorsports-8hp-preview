Family identification

Same family name. Different application details.

8HP70 is a family name, not one interchangeable transmission. Tag, part number, donor history, and drivetrain still matter.

Dodson groups 8HP65, 70, 75, and 76 component categories on one published page. That grouping does not confirm vehicle fitment or interchangeability.

Published Dodson component categories

Hardware available for the conversation.

Dodson publishes these components for the wider transmission family. C&C identifies what applies to the exact unit, vehicle, and job.

01

Clutch packages

Dodson’s current 8HP65/70/75/76 range lists SUPERSTOCK, SPORTSMAN’S, SPORTSMAN’S PLUS, and PROMAX clutch kits.

02

High-stall converter upgrades

Dodson publishes converter upgrades for this family. The engine, torque curve, tire, and launch behavior all affect the conversation.

03

Billet C and E clutch hubs

The published component range includes billet upgrades for the C and E clutch positions in this family.

04

Overhaul and workshop parts

Dodson also publishes overhaul kits and assembly tooling. The condition of the transmission decides what the job needs.

Source: Dodson 8HP65 / 70 / 75 / 76 platform page. Manufacturer component information does not establish a complete C&C transmission rating, guaranteed fitment, or parts list.

What changes the build

How the car loads the transmission.

Send the peak number, then add the details that explain how the car reaches it and how often it repeats the hit.

  1. 01

    Torque delivery

    A turbo or supercharged engine can load the transmission hard before the car reaches peak power. Include current and target torque when you have it.

  2. 02

    Tire and launch

    Vehicle weight, grip, and launch method decide how much of the hit goes into the driveline instead of spinning the tire.

  3. 03

    Repeat heat

    Street pulls, roll racing, drag passes, converter behavior, and cooling all change how quickly the transmission has to repeat a hard event.

  4. 04

    Current condition

    Mileage, service history, slip, flare, fault codes, fluid condition, and previous work can change the job before upgrades are added.

Build direction

Where does the car live?

Use profile first. Exact parts after application review.

  1. 01Street

    Road miles, street behavior, temperature, repeatability, and occasional launches

  2. 02Competition

    Regular roll or drag use, grip, repeated hard shifts, and heat recovery

  3. 03Max Effort

    Dedicated race use, high-grip launches, vehicle mass, repeated passes, and complete driveline review
